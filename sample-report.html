Fibre-to-the-premises (FTTP) is widely available in Neapolis through Cyta and Epic, with speeds of 300Mbps–1Gbps common in newer buildings. When viewing apartments, confirm fibre availability directly — older buildings may still be on VDSL.

Section 04

Housing Advice

For a 6-month furnished rental in Neapolis, expect to pay €1,000–1,300/month for a 1-bedroom. Landlords in this area are generally accustomed to digital nomads and short-term professional tenants — 6-month leases are common and negotiable.

Where to search: Bazaraki.com is the main local classifieds platform and has the most current listings. Expat Facebook groups (“Expats in Limassol”, “Digital Nomads Cyprus”) regularly have direct landlord posts without agency fees. Avoid Airbnb for 6-month stays — the pricing is 40–60% higher than long-term rental market rates for the same properties.

Tip: Request a lease that begins 1–2 weeks before your intended start date. Landlords often agree to a reduced rate for the overlap period, and it gives you time to settle before you need to be fully productive.

Section 05

Visa & Residency Overview

As a Dutch (EU) citizen, you have the right to reside in Cyprus without a visa for your full 6-month stay. Cyprus is an EU member state and freedom of movement applies fully.

For stays exceeding 3 months, you are technically required to register with the Civil Registry and Migration Department using the MEU1 form (EU citizen registration certificate). In practice, enforcement for stays under 12 months is light, but registering is straightforward, costs nothing, and protects you if questions arise. The Limassol District Office on Gladstone Street handles this — bring your passport and proof of address (a lease agreement is sufficient).

No work permit is required to work remotely for non-Cypriot clients or employers as an EU citizen.

Section 06

Tax Considerations

For a 6-month stay, Cyprus tax residency is unlikely to apply to you (it requires 183+ days in Cyprus, or 60 days under the “60-day rule” if you meet specific criteria). You will almost certainly remain a Dutch tax resident for this stay.

If you are considering making Cyprus your primary base for 12+ months in future, the picture changes significantly. Cyprus operates a Non-Domiciled (Non-Dom) resident regime that exempts qualifying individuals from tax on dividends and passive income for 17 years. The standard personal income tax rate starts at 0% on income up to €19,500 and caps at 35%. Combined with Non-Dom status, Cyprus is one of the most advantageous tax environments in the EU for self-employed and dividend-income individuals.

For your current 6-month stay: no action required. If you decide to extend, consult a Cyprus-based tax adviser before crossing the 183-day threshold.

Lifestyle Fit

Food: Limassol has the most diverse restaurant scene in Cyprus. Vegetarian and vegan options are readily available, particularly around the old town and the seafront strip. The main municipal market (Agora) on Kanningos Street is excellent for fresh produce.

Social scene: The expat and digital nomad community is concentrated in Limassol, with regular meetups organised through Meetup.com and the “Digital Nomads Cyprus” Facebook group. Beach access: The Limassol Municipal Beach is a 10-minute walk from central Neapolis. Dasoudi Beach (slightly east, quieter) is accessible by a 20-minute walk along the promenade. Water temperature remains swimmable through October.

Weather in September–February: September and October are warm and dry (28–32°C). November cools to 20–24°C — still excellent for outdoor working. December–February sees temperatures of 12–18°C with occasional rain; a light jacket is sufficient.

Section 08

Risks & Things to Watch Out For

  • Rental market tightness: Good furnished 1BRs in Neapolis move quickly. If you start your search less than 6 weeks before your move date, the best options may already be gone. Begin searching 8–10 weeks out and be prepared to commit to a lease remotely with a deposit transfer.
  • Car dependency outside Limassol: The city itself is walkable, but public transport between cities is infrequent and slow. If you plan to explore the island regularly, consider renting a car for occasional weekends rather than daily use — rates are low (€15–25/day) outside peak season.
  • Banking: Opening a Cypriot bank account as a non-resident requires in-person visits and can take 4–6 weeks. For a 6-month stay it is rarely worth the effort. Revolut and Wise are widely accepted and used by the expat community.
  • Summer hangover pricing: Some restaurants and bars on the main tourist strip inflate prices through September. The streets one block back from the seafront offer the same quality at 20–30% lower prices.
